# Severity and finding identity

Severity describes the defect's impact, not which task should be done first. Consider intensity, scope and whether a listener can naturally resolve the meaning or remain immersed.

| Level | Definition | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Clearly destroys the safe sleep experience, or makes the central experience untenable. | Evidence establishes threat, coercion or entrapment, or a core contradiction that context cannot resolve. |
| P1 | A demonstrable defect causes misunderstanding, an interruption in comprehension, conspicuous loss of immersion or local arousal, while the central experience remains viable. | Examples include meaning-changing grammar, unresolved reference, incoherent movement, conspicuous translation-like language or a new suspense thread during descent. |
| P2 | Meaning, logic and the core experience remain intact, but a demonstrable local expression or pacing defect reduces fluency. | Examples include unnecessary adjacent repetition, redundant description or mildly awkward phrasing. Equally natural alternatives are preferences, not findings. |

An isolated defect can be P0; a long repair need not be. Numerous P1 instances do not automatically become P0. A grammar problem is not automatically minor or major. “Closed,” “deep water,” and “height” alone do not establish danger: examine freedom of movement, surroundings and the described experience.

Explain severity through the quoted passage's effect. If a benign reading is established by context, do not promote a speculative harmful reading to a finding. Natural fragments and gentle ongoing movement may be appropriate.

## Types and IDs

Choose one primary type for each distinct cause; secondary labels may clarify it. Use these identifiers consistently when applicable:

- ENG: grammar, spelling, tense or reference.
- NAT: natural expression, listening comprehension or cultural association.
- SAF: safety, coercion or entrapment.
- ARO: stimulation or attention demands.
- LOG: narrative, spatial or perceptual logic.
- GEN: genre or fantasy-world consistency.
- RHY: rhythm, repetition or redundancy.

Use sequential IDs within a type (for example LOG-01). A passage is not counted multiple times merely because one defect has several effects. Related evidence may span multiple locations; list all relevant original line ranges. Independent causes may have separate findings, with coordinated repairs where their spans overlap.
