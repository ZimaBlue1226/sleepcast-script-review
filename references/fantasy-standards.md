# Secondary-world fantasy review standards

Apply the general English standards and assess the following in the actual narration:

- Impossible phenomena belong ordinarily to a concrete world with stable rules.
- The same phenomenon behaves consistently across scenes and interactions.
- Fantasy appears through perceptible scenes, characters and events rather than explanations of the author's design.
- Fantasy has enough presence to sustain the defining experience instead of disappearing into generic nature description.
- Characters, ecology and interactions support that experience.
- Actions, changes and viewpoint make the world understandable on first listening.
- Development and environmental dwelling gradually settle without erasing the fantasy itself.

Assess contradictions using all relevant passages. A rule need not be explicitly explained if its consistent behavior is perceptible. Do not invent extra world requirements or require a particular example's scenery, characters or structure. Prefer repairs that retain the central fantasy and reduce confusing or arousing presentation.
